# Initial impression, positionality and statements to prove

*Steps 1 and 2 of the synthetic reading of Charlotte Perkins Gilman's "The Yellow Wallpaper". Written by the researcher after reading three blocks (about 20% of the story) listed in `partial_reading.txt`.*

1. Initial Impression:
The sampled lines reveal a severe tension between institutional authority (medical and marital) and subjective female experience. The narrator's confinement under the guise of therapeutic 'rest' produces a psychic projection onto the room's wallpaper, transforming spatial entrapment into an eventual, radical rupture of domestic obedience.

2. Positionality:
I read "The Yellow Wallpaper" as a student of Humanities and Social Sciences at IIT (ISM) Dhanbad with a sociological background, trained to read texts as evidence of institutions, roles and power. I came to the story through feminist standpoint epistemology and the medical gaze. What I noticed first was how John's authority as both husband and physician decides what counts as the narrator's illness. My position may lead me to overlook the story's literary form, such as its irony and unreliable narration, and the details of nineteenth-century medicine. Counting vocabulary also reduces the story to word lists.

3. Research Problem:
How does institutional paternalism pathologize female creative autonomy as illness, and how does the domestic interior become a site of epistemic violence and counter-hegemonic madness?

4. Theoretical Frameworks Selected:
- The Medical Gaze & Biopolitics: Analyzing the nineteenth-century 'rest cure' as disciplinary surveillance over the female body.
- Feminist Standpoint Epistemology & Epistemic Violence: Examining the systematic silencing of the female subject's lived reality by patriarchal medical discourse.
- Subversive Hysteria (Gilbert & Gubar): Interpreting the creeping double in the wallpaper as the repressed self achieving liberation through psychological refusal.

5. Statements to Prove (written before counting; coded as H1 to H3 in `count_themes.py` and as S1 to S3 in the notebook):
- S1 / H1. Confinement and medical-gaze language is denser in the first half of the story than in the second half.
- S2 / H2. Creeping-double language becomes denser as the story moves towards its end.
- S3 / H3. Liberation language is concentrated in the last 20% of the story.
